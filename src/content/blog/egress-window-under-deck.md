You want a legal bedroom in the basement. The only wall that makes sense for the window is the one with the deck on top of it. Or the deck is already there, the old basement window sits underneath it, and you are wondering if that window can ever count as an escape route.

It can. The IRC does not ban an egress window under a deck or porch. It does set a condition, though, and that condition is about the space around the window, not the window itself. ## Why an Escape Opening Has to Lead Somewhere

An egress window is not just a hole big enough to climb through. It is the way out when the stairs are blocked, and the way in for a firefighter in gear. That only works if the opening lets someone reach open air and then get away from the house.

A window that opens onto a solid deck floor two feet above your head does not do that. Neither does a well that opens into a crawl-through gap that narrows to a few inches. The window itself can meet every number in the code and still fail the purpose.

For the basics on the window itself, see [the IRC egress window code requirements](/blog/irc-egress-window-code-requirements/). The short version: 5.7 square feet of net clear opening (5.0 at a grade-floor opening), at least 24 inches of clear height, at least 20 inches of clear width, and a sill no higher than 44 inches above the floor.

## What the IRC Says About Openings Under Decks and Porches

The model code has a specific provision for this situation, section R310.2.4, covering escape openings installed under decks and porches. The wording depends on the edition:

- The [2018 IRC text](https://up.codes/viewer/nebraska/irc-2018/chapter/3/building-planning#R310.2.4) says the opening must be fully openable and provide a path not less than 36 inches in height to a yard or court.
- The 2021 IRC adds cantilevers and a width: a path at least 36 inches high and 36 inches wide. Boulder County, Colorado, publishes that version in its [guide to emergency escape and rescue openings](https://bouldercounty.gov/property-and-land/land-use/building/building-publications/b10-emergency-escape-and-rescue-openings/).

If you are planning new work, design to 36 by 36 inches. It meets either version.

Read that carefully, because it is where most plans go wrong.

**Fully openable** means the sash has to swing or slide out of the way completely. A window that is only half open when the deck framing or a joist is in the way is not fully openable.

**The path** is a tunnel you could actually move through. Picture a box 36 inches high and 36 inches wide that starts at the window and runs out to the open yard. Joists, beams, posts, lattice, a stair stringer, a water heater vent, or a pile of lumber in that box all count against you.

Two cautions. First, section numbers and exact wording change between editions of the code, and Boulder County is a Colorado jurisdiction, not Lincoln. We are not telling you which edition applies to your address. Ask the city, or read the permit guidance on [how the Lincoln and Lancaster County egress permit works](/blog/lincoln-lancaster-county-egress-permit/). Second, a local building department can amend any of this. Confirm the path dimensions with them before you commit to a plan.

## The Window Well Adds Its Own Rules

Most basement windows in Lincoln sit below grade, which means a window well. If your window is under a deck, the well is under the deck too. The well has its own requirements, and the deck has to leave room for them.

The IRC sets these for the well:

- The well needs at least 9 square feet of horizontal area, with a projection and width of at least 36 inches.
- The well has to allow the window to fully open.
- A well deeper than 44 inches needs a permanently attached ladder or steps.
- Any cover, bar, grille or screen over the opening has to keep the required clear opening and has to release or remove from inside without a key, a tool, or more force than normal operation of the window.

That last point matters under a deck. Joists directly over the well can make a cover hard to lift or a ladder hard to climb. A person escaping in smoke needs to stand in the well, push the cover up, and climb out. If a joist sits four inches above the cover, they cannot.

For the well dimensions in more detail, see [window well size requirements in Nebraska](/blog/window-well-size-requirements-nebraska/) and [window well ladder requirements](/blog/window-well-ladder-requirements/).

## Quick Test: Does Your Deck Pass Before Anyone Calls?

You can run a rough check yourself with a tape measure. Nothing here replaces a real inspection, but it tells you which of the options below you are in.

1. **Measure from the sill up.** How much clear height is there from the top of the window well (or the ground, if there is no well yet) to the underside of the deck framing? Under about 36 inches is a problem right away.
2. **Measure out from the wall.** How far does the deck extend from the house? A deck that stops 5 feet out is a short crawl. A deck that runs 14 feet out is a long one.
3. **Look at the path.** Where does the 36 by 36 inch tunnel end up? At a yard, or at a fence, a retaining wall, a neighbor's side yard that is closed off?
4. **Look at what is in the way.** Beams, posts, footings, utility lines, A/C unit, gas meter.
5. **Check the other directions.** Could the window go on another wall instead?

If you fail at step 1, the deck is the problem. If step 1 passes and steps 3 or 4 fail, the path is the problem. Different problems have different fixes.

## Your Options

### Option 1: Leave the Window Where It Is and Make the Path Work

This works when the deck is high off the ground, the well is not deep, and you only have a few things in the way. You might be able to:

- Move a stored item or an A/C line out of the path.
- Cut a section of lattice or skirting that boxes in the space under the deck.
- Choose a window style that opens fully in a tight space. A casement or a slider can behave very differently when it is under joists.

This is the cheapest route because it changes the least. A new window in an existing opening is a different project from a cut-in. For figures, see [what egress windows cost in Lincoln](/egress-window-cost/) instead of guessing from a number in a blog post.

### Option 2: Move the Window to a Different Wall

If the deck covers one wall and the house has another wall with a clear yard on the outside, the window can go there. This is often the cleanest answer, especially for a bedroom you are creating in a finished basement. A new opening in a foundation wall is a cut-in, which is covered on our [egress window installation](/egress-window-installation-lincoln/) page.

The tradeoff is the room layout. The window has to be in the bedroom, so you may be moving a wall or changing which room becomes the bedroom.

### Option 3: Raise or Cut Back the Deck

If the deck is low and sits right over the only wall that works, the deck can change instead of the window. You can:

- Raise a section of the deck.
- Cut the deck back to leave a clear zone in front of the window.
- Rebuild a portion with a hatch or removable panel.

A deck is a structure with posts, footings, a ledger and a guard, and changing it is a carpenter's or deck builder's job. We install egress windows and window wells; we do not do structural design, and we do not do deck framing. Bring in a deck contractor first, and if the deck is attached to the house, ask whoever does the work to confirm how the ledger stays supported.

### Option 4: A Hatch or Removable Panel in the Decking

Some homeowners ask about a hinged hatch or lift-out section in the deck directly above the well. It is possible to build something like this, but it creates new questions. Who lifts it, from below, in smoke? Does it open without a key, a tool, or special knowledge? Does snow or a planter sit on top of it in January? The code language for covers, grilles and bars above is the standard to hold it to, and your building department is the one to ask if the idea would be accepted at all.

We would not choose this as a first option. A permanently clear path is simpler than a part that has to work on the worst night of the year.

### Option 5: Skip the Egress Window and Rethink the Room

If nothing above works, the basement room can still be a family room, an office or a gym. It just cannot be a legal sleeping room without egress. See [basement egress without a bedroom](/blog/basement-egress-without-bedroom/) for what that looks like,.

## Things Specific to Lincoln

Lincoln winters put a lot of snow on decks and against walls. Snow that drifts into a well under a deck has nowhere to go, and spring melt on clay soil can leave water standing. A deck also shades the well, so ice sits longer in the spring. That does not change the code, but it is worth planning for. Plan the drain before you finalize a layout, and keep grade sloping away from the house.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you put an egress window under a deck?

Yes, the IRC has a provision for it, R310.2.4. The opening has to be fully openable with a clear path at least 36 inches high to a yard or court, and the 2021 edition also requires 36 inches of width. Designing to 36 by 36 inches covers both. Your local building department decides which edition and amendments apply.

### What if the deck is too low for the 36-inch path?

Then the window as planned does not meet that provision. You can move the window to another wall, raise or cut back the deck, or change the room so it is not a sleeping room. A deck contractor handles the deck changes, and we handle the window and well.

### Does an egress window under a porch follow the same rule?

The IRC provision covers decks, porches and cantilevers together, so the same fully openable and clear path language applies to a porch. Enclosed or roofed porches can have extra framing, steps or foundation walls in the way, so measure the path before you plan.

### Can I put a cover over a window well that sits under a deck?

You can, but the cover has to keep the required opening and has to release from inside without a key, a tool, or more than normal effort. Joists close above the well can make that hard, so check the clearance.

### Will I need a permit to add or change an egress window under a deck?

Ask the city whether your project needs a permit; we pull it for our work. A Lincoln building permit is typically $75 to $200, and the cost page has the current picture. If the deck is also being altered, ask the city whether that work needs its own permit.
