A window well cover looks like a simple product, which is why it's easy to buy the wrong one. The wrong cover can leak, sag under snow, blow off in a storm, or worse, trap someone in a basement that needs an emergency exit. Most of those problems trace back to questions that never got asked at the start.

Here are the ones worth asking before you hire anyone, ours included. For each, we've included what a good answer sounds like. If you want to see how we'd answer, call [(402) 261-0485](tel:+14022610485) or [request a free written estimate](/#contact).

## 1. Will You Measure on Site?

Window wells are rarely perfectly square. Foundations settle, walls bow slightly, and older Lincoln homes have wells in odd sizes that nobody thought to standardize. A cover ordered from a number you read off with a tape measure can come in too tight, too loose or too short to clear the lip.

A good answer: yes, someone comes to the house, measures the well at the top and at the point where the cover will sit, checks for out-of-square corners, and notes anything that gets in the way, such as a drain stub, a ladder or a window that swings out. If you'd rather start yourself, our post on [how to measure a window well cover](/blog/how-to-measure-window-well-cover/) shows what to record. Expect any careful company to double-check your numbers anyway.

## 2. Does the Cover Release From the Inside?

This is the most important question if the window under the well is a bedroom or basement escape window. The 2018 IRC, which Nebraska and Lincoln use, says in R310.4 that covers, bars, grilles and screens over an escape opening must release from the inside without a key, a tool, special knowledge or more force than it takes to operate the window. You can [read the text for Nebraska](https://up.codes/viewer/nebraska/irc-2018/chapter/3/building-planning#R310.4) yourself, and the company should be able to point to it. Confirm with Lincoln Building and Safety for your address.

Ask them to show you the release. A good answer is a simple latch or a lift-up design that somebody standing in the well could figure out in the dark, in a hurry, with smoke in the room. Be wary of any answer involving a key, a padlock from outside only, or a "just unscrew it."

## 3. How Do You Size for Snow?

Lincoln gets heavy, wet snow, and drifts pile against house walls. A cover over a wide well carries real weight. Ask how the company thinks about snow load, whether the sheet is thick enough for the span, and whether the design needs a support across the middle.

A good answer talks about span, thickness and frame, not just "it's strong." And if the answer is "we add a crossbar in the middle," ask how that affects someone climbing out of the well. Our post on [window well cover snow load in Nebraska](/blog/window-well-cover-snow-load-nebraska/) goes through what to consider.

## 4. What Fasteners, Into What?

A cover is only as secure as the way it's attached. Ask:

- What kind of fasteners will you use, and are they made to resist rust?
- What are they going into: poured concrete, block, or the well itself?
- How many, and how far apart?
- What happens if the concrete around a hole is crumbling?

A good answer is specific. "Masonry anchors into the foundation, stainless or coated hardware" is a real answer. "Screws" is not. If the foundation itself is cracked or failing, the company should say so and tell you that repairing it is a different trade.

## 5. Do You Do Custom Shapes?

Plenty of wells aren't rectangles. You might have a half-round steel well, a stepped stone surround, a well with a downspout leaning on one corner, or a stacked-block well with an irregular lip. Off-the-shelf covers often don't fit those.

Ask whether the company builds to fit, what shapes and sizes they can handle, and what's different in how they'd anchor a custom cover. See our post on [custom window well covers](/blog/custom-window-well-covers/) for the kinds of situations that call for one. The answer you want is one that says how they'd handle your shape, not "no problem."

## 6. What if the Well Itself Is Rusted or Bowed?

A cover over a failing well is a short-term fix. If the wall is rusted through, pulling away from the foundation or bowing inward, the cover won't sit right, and water and soil will keep getting in.

Ask whether the company can also repair or replace the well, add a ladder if the depth calls for it, and set the gravel base and drain connection. A company that only sells covers can still be a good choice, but you'll want to know before you start whether you'll need a second contractor. ## 7. How Will You Handle the Drain?

A cover keeps leaves and snow out, but it doesn't stop water from the ground. If the well has no drain, or the drain is clogged, you'll still get water in the well. Ask whether the company checks the drain as part of the visit. Standing water speeds up rust and decay, whatever cover you choose. If the well is deeper than 44 inches, ask about the ladder too, since the code requires a permanently attached ladder or steps at that depth.

## 8. Will I Get the Price in Writing Before Work Starts?

Covers and ladders typically run $150 to $600, depending on size, material and shape. Your total depends on whether the well needs work too. A fair company puts the price in writing, describes what's included, and says what would change it.

Be careful with:

- Prices given only by phone, with no measurement.
- Quotes that don't list the material, the size or the hardware.
- A deposit request before anyone has seen your well.
- Pressure to decide on the spot.

## 9. Who Pulls the Permit, if One Is Needed?

A cover alone often doesn't need a permit, but a new well, a new window or work to an escape opening can. A Lincoln building permit typically runs $75 to $200. Ask who handles it and whether the company is comfortable explaining the requirements. We pull the permit for our own work. ## A Quick Scorecard

Take this list with you when you call around:

1. Measures on site.
2. Can show the inside release, and explains the code rule.
3. Talks about snow load in terms of span and thickness.
4. Names the fasteners and what they go into.
5. Builds custom shapes, or tells you honestly that they don't.
6. Can handle a rusted or bowed well, or tells you who can.
7. Looks at drainage.
8. Writes the price down before work begins.

If a company gets stuck on number 2, that's the one to walk away on.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a professional to install a window well cover?

Not always. A basic cover on a standard well can be a DIY job. Custom shapes, deep wells, covers over egress windows and anchoring into concrete or block are where a professional measurement and install pay off.

### Does a cover over an egress window need to open from inside?

Yes. The 2018 IRC (R310.4) requires covers over escape openings to release from the inside without keys, tools, special knowledge or more force than operating the window. Confirm local requirements with Lincoln Building and Safety.

### How much does a window well cover cost in Lincoln?

Covers and ladders typically run $150 to $600. The final number depends on size, material, shape and hardware. Our [cost page](/egress-window-cost/) puts well and cover work in context with the rest of a project.

### What if my window well is rusted?

Then the well may need repair or replacement before a cover will sit properly. Window well replacement typically runs $600 to $1,500. Ask any company you consider whether they can also handle the well.

### Should I get more than one estimate?

It's a good idea, as long as each estimate is in writing and lists the material, size, hardware and what happens with the drain. Comparing like for like is what makes the numbers useful.
