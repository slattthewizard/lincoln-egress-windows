# Window Well Cover Repair: What Can Be Fixed and What Needs Replacing

A cover that rattles in the wind, a hinge that won't hold, a sheet that sags in the middle after a snowy week. Most window well cover problems start small, and a few of them really are a Saturday-morning fix. Others mean the cover has reached the end of its useful life, and patching it only buys you a few months.

This guide walks through the common failures, which ones you can handle with basic tools, which ones call for a new cover, and the one rule that applies to every repair on a basement window well in Lincoln: the cover still has to open from the inside. ## Start With the Egress Rule

Before you touch a screw, know what the cover is for. If the window under it is your basement's emergency escape opening, the cover is part of that escape route. The 2018 IRC, which Nebraska and Lincoln use, says in section R310.4 that bars, grilles, covers and screens over an escape opening must release from the inside without a key, a tool, special knowledge, or more force than it takes to operate the window itself. You can read the [2018 IRC text for Nebraska](https://up.codes/viewer/nebraska/irc-2018/chapter/3/building-planning#R310.4) yourself. Confirm with Lincoln Building and Safety for your address.

That rule shapes what counts as a safe repair:

- Don't fix a loose cover by screwing it shut from the outside or running a lock through the frame.
- Don't add a hasp, padlock or hidden latch that someone in a smoky basement wouldn't figure out in seconds.
- Don't weigh a cover down with blocks, bricks or a heavy planter to stop it blowing off.

If a repair makes the cover harder to lift from the well side, it's the wrong repair, even if it solves the rattle. For the full picture on covers over escape windows, see our page on [window well covers in Lincoln](/window-well-covers-lincoln/).

## Fixes You Can Usually Do Yourself

### Loose or missing fasteners

Screws and anchors into a concrete foundation or the top of a block wall work loose over time. Freeze-thaw cycles do it, and so does the constant push and pull of wind on a flat cover. If a screw spins without biting, the hole is probably stripped or the anchor is cracked.

Try this first: pull the old fastener, clean the hole, and set a new masonry anchor in a fresh spot a short distance away. Don't just jam a bigger screw into a crumbling hole. Use stainless or coated hardware, because plain steel will rust and stain the frame. If the foundation concrete around the hole is crumbling, stop and look at the wall. That's a foundation question, and it's outside what we do.

### Bent hinges

A hinge that has folded slightly out of true can sometimes be straightened. Take the cover off, lay the hinge on a flat surface and work it back with pliers or a hammer. If the metal has cracked, or the hinge leaf has torn away from the cover, replace the hinge. Hinges are cheap. Just match the hole spacing, and keep the hinge on the side where the cover can still be pushed up from inside the well.

### Broken prop arms and latches

Many covers use a prop arm to hold the lid open while you clean the well. If the arm has snapped or the pin walked out, a replacement part from the cover's maker is the clean answer. A handyman-store arm that doesn't fit the pivot points often does more harm than good.

Latches deserve extra care. A simple spring latch that you can slide with your fingers from inside the well is fine. A latch that needs a key, a screwdriver, or a trick to release is not. If a latch has broken, replace it with the same style of release, or choose one that works by pushing up from below.

### Small chips on the edge

A small chip on the edge of a polycarbonate or acrylic cover that doesn't run into the sheet is mostly cosmetic. Sand the edge lightly so it doesn't catch skin, and keep an eye on it. A chip that stays put is fine. A chip that grows is a crack in the making.

## A Cover That Blew Off

Spring storms in Lincoln can lift a light cover right off its frame. If yours landed in the yard, check three things before putting it back:

1. **The cover itself.** Look for cracks that spread from the corners or from the fastener holes, bent frame rails, and torn hinge leaves. A bent frame rarely goes back true.
2. **The mounting.** Did the fasteners pull out of the wall, or did the cover rip away from them? If the concrete or block is damaged, the repair is bigger than a cover.
3. **The fit.** If the cover was sized loosely or sat on a lip that has since shifted, it'll blow off again. See [how to measure a window well cover](/blog/how-to-measure-window-well-cover/) before you decide to reuse it.

If the cover is intact and the mounting is solid, re-anchoring it with proper hardware is a fair fix. If the cover has cracked or twisted, plan on a replacement. We'll get to that below.

## Sagging Sheets

A sheet that sags in the middle is under more load than it was built for, or the material has aged and gone soft. Snow is the usual cause. A wet, heavy snowfall on a wide span puts real weight on the middle of the cover. If the sheet has flexed once and sprung back, it may be fine. If it stays bowed, it has been stretched past its limit.

You can't really repair a permanent sag. Adding a center support under the cover helps for some wells, but it also puts a post or crossbar in the way of anyone climbing out. That conflicts with the escape rule, so we'd treat a persistent sag as a sign to replace. ## Cracks and Yellowed or Brittle Plastic

Here the repair advice gets short. A hairline crack in clear plastic will usually keep growing, because every freeze and every gust flexes the sheet and pulls the crack along. Glue, tape and sealant tend to hold for a while and then let go. Because the cover is also what keeps a child or a pet out of the well, a cracked sheet isn't one to gamble on.

Yellowing and a chalky surface point to years of sun. Polycarbonate and acrylic both age in UV light, and the surface gets hazy and then brittle. Brittle plastic can shatter instead of flexing. If you can flake the surface with a fingernail, or a gentle press makes it crackle, replace it. ## Rusted Steel Grates

Steel grate covers rust. Light surface rust on a sound grate can be wire-brushed, treated with a rust converter and finished with a coat of exterior metal paint. Do it before the rust reaches the welds.

Replace the grate when you see any of these:

- Flaking rust that has thinned the bars or left holes.
- Cracked or rusted-through welds.
- Rust that has eaten the hinge pins or latch.
- A frame that has twisted so the grate no longer sits flat.

A rusted grate is also a clue about the well. If the cover has rusted badly, the galvanized well wall beneath it may have been taking the same punishment. Take a look at the well itself.

## When the Well Is the Problem

Sometimes the cover isn't failing. The well is moving under it. A bowed wall, a well pulling away from the foundation, or a well that has shifted will make a cover that fit last year sit crooked this year. No amount of cover repair fixes that. ## Repair or Replace: A Quick Way to Decide

**Repair it** when the sheet or grate is sound and the problem is hardware: loose screws, a bent hinge, a missing prop arm, a latch that still releases from inside.

**Replace it** when the sheet is cracked, yellowed to the point of brittleness, permanently sagging, or the frame is bent. Replace it too when you can't get a repair to release easily from inside the well.

**Look at the well** when the cover keeps coming loose no matter how you fix it, or when the cover no longer fits the opening.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I fix a cracked window well cover with glue or tape?

A patch might hold for a short time, but the crack usually keeps traveling as the sheet flexes with wind and temperature swings. It also leaves a weak spot over a hole in the ground. A cracked sheet is usually better replaced.

### Is it safe to screw or lock a window well cover shut?

Not over an egress window. The 2018 IRC (R310.4) requires covers to release from inside without keys, tools, special knowledge or extra force. A lock or screws from outside can trap someone in an emergency. Ask Lincoln Building and Safety if you're unsure about your address.

### Why does my window well cover keep blowing off?

Usually the cover is too light for the span, the fit is loose, or the fasteners have pulled out of the foundation. Fix the mounting and the fit rather than weighing the cover down, and make sure whatever you do still lets the cover open from inside.

### Can a rusted steel grate be saved?

Light surface rust can be brushed, treated and repainted. If the bars are thin, the welds are cracked or the hinges have rusted through, replace the grate.
