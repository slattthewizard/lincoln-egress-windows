## Window Size vs. Window Well Size Requirements: Two Different Rules

The International Residential Code treats the window opening and the well around it as two separate items in the same code section, each checked on its own. Lincoln and Lancaster County enforce both.

For contrast, here's what the window itself needs: a net clear opening of at least 5.7 square feet, a minimum clear height of 24 inches, a minimum clear width of 20 inches, and a sill no higher than 44 inches above the finished floor. Those numbers are about the opening you climb through.

Window well size requirements are about something different: the space you land in before you can climb out at all. A window can hit every one of those four numbers and still be sitting in a well that's too narrow, too shallow, or too close to the foundation to count as a legal exit path.

That's the gap that catches people off guard. Nobody assumes their window is fine and their well isn't, because most people think of the two as one unit. Code doesn't see it that way.

## Window Well Size Requirements: The Numbers That Actually Apply

A window well serving an egress window needs at least 9 square feet of clear horizontal area. That's the floor space inside the well, not the size of the cover on top or the depth of the pit.

Two more numbers narrow that further. The well needs a minimum horizontal projection of 36 inches out from the foundation wall, and a minimum width of 36 inches. Both dimensions have to clear 36 inches at the same time, just like the window's own height and width have to clear their minimums together.

A well can hit 9 square feet on paper and still fail if it's shaped wrong. A long, narrow well that's 60 inches wide but only 20 inches deep front to back has plenty of area but fails the 36 inch projection rule outright. The math and the shape both have to work.

| Requirement | The Window Opening | The Window Well |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum clear area | 5.7 sq ft net clear | 9 sq ft clear area |
| Minimum width | 20 in clear width | 36 in width |
| Projection from foundation | Not applicable | 36 in minimum |
| Height or depth rule | 44 in maximum sill height | Deeper than 44 in needs a ladder or steps |
| What it measures | The opening you climb through | The space you climb into first |

Once you see the two side by side, the pattern is clear. The window rules cap the sill and set a floor for width and height. The well rules set a floor for area, width, and projection, with a separate depth trigger for a ladder. They're checked on different tape measure marks, and neither one substitutes for the other.

## Ladders, Steps, and the Well's Clear Opening

Window well size requirements don't stop at square footage. Depth brings in a second rule that trips up homeowners who solve one problem and accidentally create another.

Any well deeper than 44 inches, measured from the well floor to the ground surface outside it, needs a permanently affixed ladder or built-in steps. That's a separate trigger from the 9 square foot area rule, and a well can pass on size while still needing a ladder because of its depth.

Here's the part that trips up DIYers: the ladder or steps can't obstruct the well's required clear opening. A ladder mounted where it eats into the 36 inch width, or blocks the window from swinging fully open, fails inspection even if the well's raw dimensions are correct on paper. The ladder has to fit inside the well's footprint without shrinking the usable escape space below the minimums.

This is exactly why enlarging a well sometimes means going a little bigger than the bare 9 square foot minimum, especially if the well is also deep enough to need a ladder. A well built right at the minimum with no room to spare for a ladder usually needs a second round of work later. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum size for a window well in Nebraska?

A window well serving an egress window needs at least 9 square feet of clear horizontal area, with a minimum width of 36 inches and a minimum horizontal projection of 36 inches out from the foundation. All three numbers have to be met at the same time. This is separate from the window opening's own size requirements.

### Can my window well be too small even if my egress window is the right size?

Yes, and it's one of the most common reasons a basement bedroom fails inspection. The window opening and the well around it are measured against completely different numbers, so a code-compliant window can still sit in a well that falls short on area, width, or projection.

### How much does it cost to fix an undersized window well?

A standard well replacement around an existing, already-compliant window typically runs $600 to $1,500. Costs rise to $3,500 to $7,000, or up to $9,500 with finishing, only if the foundation opening itself also needs to be enlarged or moved, not just the well around it.

### Do I need a ladder if I enlarge my window well?

Only if the finished well is deeper than 44 inches from the well floor to the ground surface, in which case a permanently affixed ladder or steps is required. The ladder or steps must fit inside the well without blocking the required clear width or interfering with the window's full swing. Lincoln and Lancaster County confirm the exact depth trigger on the permit.
