# Toads, Mice and Rabbits in Window Wells: Keeping Animals Out

Look down into a window well after a rain and you may see a toad sitting on the gravel looking back at you. Look again in the fall and there might be a mouse, a vole, or a young rabbit that wandered too close to the edge and couldn't get back out.

## Why Window Wells Catch Animals

Corrugated galvanized steel wells have ridges, but they're too shallow and slick for most small animals to climb. Composite and plastic wells are smoother still. The wall is higher than a toad can jump or a mouse can scale, and the top edge often curls outward or sits flush with the grass.

Animals walk along the foundation edge, following cover and warmth. An open well is a hole right in their path. They drop in, and there's no way up.

Common visitors include:

- **Toads and frogs**, drawn by damp ground and the insects living in leaf litter.
- **Mice and voles**, which run along foundations and fall in.
- **Rabbits**, especially young ones, which wander at dusk.
- **Snakes**, which follow prey into the well or end up there while hunting.
- **Insects, spiders and other small creatures**, which the larger animals follow.

Fall leaves make it worse. Debris piles up and becomes a nesting layer and a food source. See [window well leaf and debris cleanup](/blog/window-well-leaf-debris-cleanup/) for how to keep that under control.

## Why It Matters Beyond the Animal

Trapped animals die in the well, and a dead animal in a basement window well smells. Droppings and carcasses can end up in the gap between the well and the window frame. Rodents also use gaps at the top of a well, and between the well and the foundation, to get to the window frame itself.

Mice can squeeze through very small openings. A well that is pulled away from the wall, a damaged window frame, rotted wood around the sill, or a cracked pane gives them a way in. If you've been seeing droppings in the basement, the well is worth checking.

None of this means your house is infested. It means the well is a place animals gather, and the more that gather there, the more likely one finds a way in.

## How to Rescue an Animal From a Window Well

If something is already down there, helping it out is simple, and most animals recover fine.

1. **Wear gloves.** Even with a toad, use thick gardening gloves or a cloth. Don't handle wild animals bare-handed.
2. **Give it a ramp.** Lean a board or a rough branch into the well at a shallow angle so the animal can walk up. A rough or textured surface works better than a slick one. A scrap of wood with a few strips of tape or a piece of old carpet stapled on gives better grip.
3. **Or use a bucket.** Lower a bucket, lean it on its side so the animal walks in, then lift it out slowly and tip it onto the grass.
4. **Back off.** Give the animal room and time. Don't corner it or reach for it.
5. **Leave the ramp overnight.** Many small animals move at night, so leave the board in place and check in the morning.

Snakes are a different matter. If you can't tell whether a snake is harmless, don't put your hand in. Use a long pole or a bucket, or call someone qualified. For anything injured, aggressive or hard to identify, call a local wildlife rehabilitator or animal control.

## Keep Them Out in the First Place: Covers Are the Real Fix

Rescue is a patch. The lasting answer is a cover. A cover closes the pit so animals don't fall in, and it also keeps out leaves, snow and debris.

Look at [our window well covers](/window-well-covers-lincoln/) for the types we work with. In general:

- **Bubble or domed covers** keep animals out and let light in, as long as they sit snug on the rim.
- **Flat clear covers** work just as well but need a tight fit and a clean edge.
- **Metal grate covers** stop rabbits and bigger animals, but toads, mice and snakes can slip through the openings. If small animals are the problem, a solid or clear cover does a better job.

## Check the Gaps

A cover is only as good as its fit. Walk around the well and look for:

- **A gap between the cover and the well rim.** A mouse can use a gap smaller than you'd think. The cover should sit snug on all sides.
- **A gap between the well and the foundation wall.** If the well has pulled away from the wall, soil, water and animals can get behind it.
- **Cracked or rotted trim around the window frame.** Wood damage gives rodents an easy way in. Rotted sill and frame repair is part of what we handle around basement windows.
- **Failed caulk or sealant** at the window frame.
- **Bent or rusted metal** in the well wall, where animals can squeeze through.

A cover that doesn't fit can be worse than no cover, because animals nest under it and it feels sheltered. Measure the well carefully before you buy. If it's an odd size, a custom cover is the better route.

## Don't Trade Animal Safety for Escape Safety

If the window is an egress window, the cover has to work for people, too. The 2018 IRC (R310.4) says bars, grilles, covers and screens over an escape opening must release from inside without a key, a tool, special knowledge, or more force than it takes to open the window itself.

That rules out padlocks, screwed-down covers and anything that someone in a smoke-filled basement couldn't pop open. A cover that keeps toads out and traps your family in is a bad trade. If the well also has a ladder, the ladder must stay reachable and the cover must lift clear of it.

## Other Things That Make Wells Less Attractive

- **Keep the area around the well clear.** Trim grass and shrubs back from the rim and remove leaf piles. Long grass gives animals cover to approach.
- **Fix standing water.** A well that holds water attracts toads, frogs and insects. Check the drain and the gravel base. Our [window well drainage maintenance checklist](/blog/window-well-drainage-maintenance-checklist/) covers what to look at.
- **Clean out debris** in spring and fall.
- **Watch the grade.** Soil should slope away from the foundation, not toward the well.
- **Don't leave food out near the house.** Pet dishes and bird feeders near the foundation bring rodents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I get a toad out of a window well?

Wear gloves and lean a board or rough branch into the well so the toad can walk up, or use a bucket lowered on its side and lifted out slowly. Then back off and let it leave on its own.

### Will a window well cover keep mice out?

A snug cover helps keep animals from falling in, but mice can use small gaps. Check the fit around the rim, the gap between the well and the wall, and the window frame. A cover doesn't replace sealing a damaged frame.

### Can I lock a window well cover to keep animals and kids out?

Not on an egress window. Covers have to release from inside without a key, tool, special knowledge or extra force. Pick a cover that fits tightly and opens easily from below.

### Is a snake in my window well dangerous?

Often not, but you can't always tell. Don't reach in. Use a pole or bucket to get it out, or call animal control or a wildlife professional.
