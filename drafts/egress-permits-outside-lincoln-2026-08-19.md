# Egress Window Permits Outside Lincoln: Waverly to Beatrice

The dimensional requirements for an egress window don't change when you drive out of Lincoln. **5.7 square feet** of net clear opening, **24 inches** minimum height, **20 inches** minimum width, a sill no higher than **44 inches** off the floor. Those are the same in Beatrice as they are on South Street.

What changes is the paperwork: who issues the permit, what it costs, how long review takes, who inspects, and whether your address is inside a city's jurisdiction at all. Getting that wrong doesn't usually mean redoing the window. It means a delay, and occasionally an awkward conversation about work that was already done.

## Why the Code Is the Same but the Process Isn't

Nebraska jurisdictions build on the International Residential Code, which is where the egress requirements come from. That's why the numbers above hold everywhere, and why [IRC egress window code requirements](/blog/irc-egress-window-code-requirements/) and [egress window size requirements in Nebraska](/blog/egress-window-size-requirements-nebraska/) apply regardless of which town you're in.

What each jurisdiction controls is administration: which code edition they've adopted, any local amendments, permit fees, submission requirements, review time, and inspection scheduling.

The practical consequences:

**Fees differ.** In Lincoln, a permit for a new foundation opening runs **$75 to $200**. Other jurisdictions set their own, and it's not safe to assume the figure transfers. Confirm with the issuing office.

**Review time differs.** Larger departments often have a faster, more routine process for something as standard as a residential egress window. Smaller offices may batch work or have limited hours, which can extend a timeline. Our [installation timeline guide](/blog/egress-window-installation-timeline/) covers the overall project length; permitting is the part most likely to vary by location.

**Inspection scheduling differs.** How much notice an inspector needs, and how quickly they can get out, varies. In a smaller jurisdiction this can be quicker in practice, since the inspector may be scheduling their own week.

**Submission requirements differ.** Some offices want a drawing showing the opening location, dimensions, and the header or lintel detail. Others accept less. For a [block foundation](/blog/block-vs-poured-foundation-egress/), where the lintel is structural, expect more scrutiny wherever you are.

## City Limits Versus County

This is the single most common source of confusion, and it's worth checking rather than assuming.

A mailing address that says "Seward, NE" doesn't mean the property is inside the City of Seward. Acreages and rural properties with a town's mailing address are frequently in the unincorporated county, permitted by the county rather than the city.

How to resolve it:

- Check your property tax statement, which identifies the taxing jurisdictions
- Call the county assessor's office with the address
- Call the city building department and ask directly, which is usually the fastest path

Getting this right matters because applying to the wrong office wastes time, and because occasionally neither office is the one you expected. It's the first thing we establish on any project outside Lincoln proper.

## What Doesn't Change Anywhere

Regardless of jurisdiction:

**The opening dimensions.** All four minimums, met simultaneously, measured as net clear opening with the window fully open, not as the size of the unit.

**Window well requirements.** Where a well is used: **9 square feet** of area with a minimum **36-inch** horizontal projection, and a permanently affixed ladder or steps where the well is deeper than **44 inches**. See [window well ladder requirements](/blog/window-well-ladder-requirements/) and [do I need a window well](/blog/do-i-need-a-window-well/).

**That sleeping rooms need an escape opening serving that room.** Covered in [what counts as a legal bedroom in Nebraska](/blog/what-counts-as-a-legal-bedroom-nebraska/) and, for basements with a door, [walkout and daylight basement egress](/blog/walkout-daylight-basement-egress/).

**That a new foundation opening is permitted work.** Cutting into a foundation wall is structural. No jurisdiction treats it as a repair you can do quietly.

**Utility locates before excavation.** Free, required, and not jurisdiction-dependent in any way that helps you.

**The consequences of skipping the permit.** Unpermitted structural work surfaces at sale, complicates insurance conversations, and leaves you with no independent verification that the header or lintel was done correctly. ## What Also Varies: The Ground Itself

Permits aren't the only thing that changes as you move around southeast Nebraska. Soil and water conditions differ enough to matter for the well and drainage design.

**Properties near the Platte**, including around Ashland, can have higher water tables and sandier soils, which changes excavation and makes drainage design more important, not less.

**Acreages and rural properties** frequently have no municipal storm system to work with, so the well drain has to be resolved on site, typically tying into the footing drain and sump or to a suitable daylight outlet.

**Older housing stock** in Crete, Seward, and Beatrice includes plenty of block foundations, which brings the lintel question in [block versus poured foundations](/blog/block-vs-poured-foundation-egress/) and the age-related conditions in [egress window retrofits in older Lincoln homes](/blog/egress-window-retrofit-older-lincoln-homes/).

Whatever the jurisdiction, drainage is what determines whether you end up with [a flooded window well](/blog/flooded-window-well/) the first big spring rain. [How window well drainage works](/blog/how-window-well-drainage-works/) covers the design.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a permit for an egress window outside Lincoln?

Yes. Cutting a new opening in a foundation wall is structural work and requires a permit throughout southeast Nebraska. What changes is which office issues it: the city building department if you're inside city limits, or the county if the property is in an unincorporated area.

### Are egress window requirements different in Beatrice or Seward than in Lincoln?

The dimensional requirements are the same, because they come from the International Residential Code: **5.7 square feet** net clear opening, **24 inches** minimum height, **20 inches** minimum width, and a **44-inch** maximum sill height. What differs is permit fees, review time, submission requirements, and inspection scheduling.

### How do I know if my property is inside city limits?

Check your property tax statement for the taxing jurisdictions listed, call the county assessor with the address, or call the city building department and ask. A town mailing address does not confirm you're inside that town's limits, which catches a lot of acreage owners.
